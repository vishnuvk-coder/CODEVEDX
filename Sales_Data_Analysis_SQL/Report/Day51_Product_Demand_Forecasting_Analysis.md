📊 Day 51 — Product Demand & Sales Forecasting Analysis
Introduction

Day 51 focuses on product-level demand analysis and sales forecasting using MySQL.

Day 50 analyzed overall sales revenue forecasting. Day 51 extends this analysis to individual products by examining product demand, product revenue, monthly units sold, demand growth, moving averages, demand trends, product rankings, and simple future demand projections.

The objective is to understand which products generate strong demand and how historical product demand can be used to create a simple baseline projection for the next month.

Objectives

The main objectives of Day 51 are:

Analyze total units sold by product.
Analyze total revenue generated by each product.
Analyze monthly product demand.
Analyze monthly product revenue.
Compare current product demand with the previous month.
Calculate month-over-month product demand growth.
Calculate 3-month moving average demand.
Classify product demand trends.
Rank products according to total units sold.
Rank products according to revenue.
Estimate next-month product demand.
Create a final product demand forecasting summary.

💰 Revenue Calculation

Product revenue is calculated using:

Product Revenue = Quantity × Product Price

SQL expression:

oi.quantity * p.price

The project uses products.price because the confirmed order_items table does not contain a separate price column.

📊 Analyses Performed
1. Product-Wise Total Units Sold

This analysis calculates the total number of units sold for each product.

It helps identify products with higher overall sales demand.

Business Question

Which products have the highest total unit sales?

2. Product-Wise Total Revenue

This analysis calculates the total revenue generated by each product.

Business Question

Which products contribute the most revenue?

Revenue is calculated using:

quantity × product price
3. Monthly Product Demand

This analysis calculates the number of units sold by each product for every available month.

This creates a product-level time-series view of demand.

Business Question

How does product demand change from month to month?

4. Monthly Product Revenue

This analysis calculates revenue generated by each product for each month.

Business Question

How does product revenue change over time?

5. Previous-Month Product Demand

The LAG() window function is used to retrieve the previous available monthly demand for each product.

Example:

LAG(monthly_units_sold) OVER (
    PARTITION BY product_id
    ORDER BY sales_month
)
Business Question

How many units did the product sell in the previous available month?

6. Month-over-Month Product Demand Growth

This analysis compares current product demand with previous-month demand.

Two metrics are calculated:

Demand change
Demand growth percentage
Formula
Demand Change =
Current Month Units - Previous Month Units
Demand Growth % =
(Current Month Units - Previous Month Units)
/
Previous Month Units × 100

NULLIF() is used to avoid division-by-zero errors.

Business Question

Which products are experiencing increasing or decreasing demand?

7. Three-Month Moving Average Product Demand

A three-month moving average is calculated for each product.

The calculation uses:

AVG(monthly_units_sold) OVER (
    PARTITION BY product_id
    ORDER BY sales_month
    ROWS BETWEEN 2 PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW
)

The moving average helps smooth short-term fluctuations in product demand.

Business Question

What is the recent average demand for each product?

8. Product Demand Trend Classification

Products are classified according to their month-over-month demand growth.

The classifications are:

Condition	Demand Trend
First available month	Baseline Month
Growth > 5%	Strong Growth
Growth > 0%	Growth
Growth = 0%	Stable
Growth >= -5%	Decline
Growth < -5%	Strong Decline

These thresholds are project-specific business-analysis rules and can be adjusted depending on the business context.

Business Question

What is the current demand trend for each product?

9. Product Demand Ranking

Products are ranked according to their total units sold.

The analysis uses:

RANK() OVER (
    ORDER BY total_units_sold DESC
)
Business Question

Which products have the highest overall demand?

10. Product Revenue Ranking

Products are ranked according to total revenue.

The analysis uses:

RANK() OVER (
    ORDER BY total_revenue DESC
)
Business Question

Which products generate the highest total revenue?

11. Next-Month Product Demand Projection

A simple next-month product demand projection is calculated using the recent three-month average.

Projection Method
Projected Next-Month Units
=
Recent 3-Month Average Units Sold

The result is rounded upward using CEIL() to provide a whole-unit projection.

Business Question

What could be used as a simple baseline estimate for next-month product demand?

12. Final Product Demand Forecasting Summary

The final analysis combines multiple product-level metrics:

Latest-month units
Previous-month units
Latest-month revenue
Demand growth percentage
Recent three-month average demand
Projected next-month units
Demand status

This provides a consolidated view of current product performance and baseline future demand.

These classifications are used for analytical interpretation and are not statistical forecasting categories.

🔮 Forecasting Method

Day 51 uses a simple SQL-based baseline forecasting method.

The projected demand is calculated using the recent three-month average:

Projected Next-Month Units
=
Average Units Sold During Recent 3 Months

For example, if a product sold:

Month 1 → 20 units
Month 2 → 25 units
Month 3 → 30 units

then:

3-Month Average
= (20 + 25 + 30) / 3
= 25 units

The baseline next-month projection would therefore be approximately 25 units.

This method is useful for demonstrating forecasting concepts with SQL, but it is not a statistical or machine-learning forecasting model.

⚠️ Forecasting Limitation

The forecasting approach used in Day 51 is intentionally simple.

It uses historical averages rather than:

Seasonality models
Regression
ARIMA
Exponential smoothing
Machine learning
External demand factors
Marketing campaigns
Pricing changes
Inventory constraints

Therefore, the projected values should be interpreted as baseline estimates for SQL business analysis, not as production-grade forecasts.
