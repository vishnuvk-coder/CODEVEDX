## 1. Introduction

Customer retention is an important business metric because acquiring a new customer can be more expensive than retaining an existing customer.

In this analysis, customer cohort analysis is used to understand how customers behave after their first purchase. Customers are grouped into cohorts according to the month of their first purchase, and their subsequent purchasing activity is tracked over time.

This analysis helps identify:

* Customer retention patterns
* Repeat purchasing behavior
* Month-by-month customer activity
* Cohort revenue performance
* Strong and weak customer cohorts
* Retention opportunities
* Potential areas for customer engagement strategies

The analysis is performed using SQL on the `sales_analysis_db` database.

# 4. What is Cohort Analysis?

Cohort analysis groups customers who share a common characteristic or event.

In this project, the common event is the customer's **first purchase**.

For example:

| Customer   | First Purchase | Cohort        |
| ---------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| Customer A | January 2025   | January 2025  |
| Customer B | February 2025  | February 2025 |
| Customer C | February 2025  | February 2025 |
| Customer D | March 2025     | March 2025    |

Customers from the same first-purchase month belong to the same cohort.

After creating cohorts, their activity can be tracked during subsequent months.

---

# 5. Cohort Retention Concept

Retention measures how many customers from an original cohort continue purchasing in later periods.

For example:

| Cohort        | Month 0 | Month 1 | Month 2 | Month 3 |
| ------------- | ------: | ------: | ------: | ------: |
| January 2025  |    100% |     45% |     32% |     25% |
| February 2025 |    100% |     51% |     36% |     28% |
| March 2025    |    100% |     39% |     27% |     21% |

Month 0 represents the customer's initial cohort month.

A decreasing percentage over time indicates customer drop-off, while stronger percentages indicate better retention.

# 6. Analysis 1 — Customer Cohort Identification

### Objective

Identify each customer's first purchase date and assign the customer to a cohort month.

### SQL Approach

The `MIN()` function is used to find the earliest order date for each customer.

The first purchase date is then converted into a month-level cohort.

### Business Purpose

This creates the foundation for all subsequent cohort and retention analysis.

### Key Insight

Every customer is assigned to the month in which they first purchased.

# 9. Analysis 4 — Cohort Retention Rate

### Objective

Calculate the percentage of customers from each cohort who remain active during subsequent months.

### Formula

```text
Retention Rate =
Active Customers / Original Cohort Customers × 100
```

### Business Purpose

Retention rate is one of the most important metrics in cohort analysis.

A higher retention rate indicates stronger customer engagement and repeat purchasing.

### Business Application

The business can compare retention performance between cohorts and investigate why some cohorts perform better than others.

---

# 10. Analysis 5 — Month-1 Retention

### Objective

Measure the percentage of customers who return during the month immediately following their first purchase.

### Metric

**Month-1 Retention %**

### Business Purpose

Month-1 retention provides an early indication of whether newly acquired customers are likely to return.

### Business Application

A low Month-1 retention rate may indicate:

* Weak customer engagement
* Poor follow-up
* Product dissatisfaction
* Lack of incentives
* Weak onboarding experience

# 11. Analysis 6 — Month-2 Retention

# 12. Analysis 7 — Month-3 Retention

# 13. Analysis 8 — Cohort Revenue Analysis

### Objective

Calculate the total revenue generated by each customer cohort.

### Revenue Calculation

```sql
oi.quantity * p.price
```

### Metrics

* Cohort month
* Total cohort revenue

### Business Purpose

Customer count alone does not show the financial value of a cohort.

Two cohorts may have similar customer counts but generate very different revenue.

### Business Application

Revenue-based cohort analysis helps identify financially valuable customer groups.

---

# 14. Analysis 9 — Cohort Repeat Purchase Analysis

### Objective

Compare one-time customers and repeat customers within each cohort.

### Customer Categories

**One-Time Customer**

A customer who placed only one order.

**Repeat Customer**

A customer who placed more than one order.

### Key Metric

```text
Repeat Customer % =
Repeat Customers / Total Customers × 100
```

### Business Purpose

This analysis identifies cohorts with stronger repeat purchasing behavior.

### Business Application

Cohorts with high repeat-purchase percentages can provide useful benchmarks for customer retention strategies.

---

# 15. Analysis 10 — Cohort Performance Ranking

### Objective

Rank customer cohorts according to their total revenue.

### SQL Concept

`RANK() OVER (ORDER BY total_revenue DESC)`

### Business Purpose

Ranking allows management to quickly identify the highest-value cohorts.

### Business Application

High-revenue cohorts can be studied to understand:

* Customer behavior
* Product preferences
* Purchasing frequency
* Revenue contribution
* Retention patterns

---

# 16. Analysis 11 — Best & Worst Performing Cohorts

### Objective

Classify cohorts according to their retention and revenue performance.

### Performance Categories

* High Performing Cohort
* Average Performing Cohort
* Low Performing Cohort

### High Performing Cohort

A cohort is considered high performing when it demonstrates strong repeat purchasing and above-average revenue.

### Low Performing Cohort

A cohort is considered low performing when it has weak repeat purchasing and below-average revenue.

### Business Purpose

This analysis helps management identify which customer cohorts should be studied, protected, or targeted for improvement.

---

# 17. Analysis 12 — Final Cohort Retention Business Summary

The final analysis combines:

* Cohort month
* Total customers
* Repeat customers
* Repeat customer percentage
* Total revenue
* Revenue per customer
* Retention category

### Retention Categories

**Strong Retention**

Repeat customer percentage is at least 50%.

**Moderate Retention**

Repeat customer percentage is between 25% and 50%.

**Weak Retention**

Repeat customer percentage is below 25%.

These thresholds are business-analysis rules used for classification and can be adjusted according to the organization's retention targets.

# 20. Business Recommendations

### 1. Improve Early Customer Engagement

Focus on customers during the first month after acquisition because early retention is important for developing repeat purchasing behavior.

### 2. Study High-Performing Cohorts

Analyze the purchasing behavior of high-performing cohorts and use those patterns as benchmarks.

### 3. Target Low-Retention Cohorts

Customers showing early inactivity can be targeted with:

* Personalized offers
* Email campaigns
* Product recommendations
* Loyalty programs
* Follow-up communication

### 4. Encourage Repeat Purchases

Businesses can introduce:

* Discounts for second purchases
* Loyalty rewards
* Bundled products
* Personalized recommendations

### 5. Monitor Cohort Revenue

Customer count should not be the only performance metric. Revenue per customer should also be monitored.

### 6. Track Retention Continuously

Cohort retention should be monitored regularly to detect changes in customer behavior.
