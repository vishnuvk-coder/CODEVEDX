# 📊 Day 36 — Customer Lifetime Value & Revenue Contribution Analysis

## 1. Introduction

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) is an important business metric used to understand the total revenue generated by a customer throughout their relationship with a business.

On Day 36 of the **Sales Data Analysis Using SQL** project, customer-level revenue and purchasing behavior were analyzed to identify high-value customers, understand customer contribution to total revenue, measure customer lifetime duration, and classify customers based on their overall value.

The analysis uses SQL techniques such as:

* Aggregate Functions
* `GROUP BY`
* `JOIN`
* Common Table Expressions (CTEs)
* Window Functions
* `DENSE_RANK()`
* `SUM() OVER()`
* `MIN()`
* `MAX()`
* `COUNT(DISTINCT)`
* `DATEDIFF()`
* `CASE`
* Subqueries
* Revenue Contribution Analysis

The analysis calculates customer revenue using:

**Customer Revenue = Quantity × Product Price**

where product price is obtained from the `products` table.

# 5. Analysis 1 — Customer Lifetime Revenue

### Objective

Calculate the total revenue generated by each customer across all of their purchases.

### SQL Logic

```text
Customer Lifetime Revenue
= SUM(Order Quantity × Product Price)
```

### Business Purpose

This metric identifies customers who have generated the greatest amount of revenue throughout their purchasing history.

### Business Insight

Customers with higher lifetime revenue represent important customers for the business. They can be targeted with loyalty programs, personalized offers, premium services, and retention campaigns.

---

# 6. Analysis 2 — Customer Total Orders

### Objective

Calculate the number of unique orders placed by every customer.

The analysis uses:

```sql
COUNT(DISTINCT order_id)
```

### Business Purpose

Order frequency helps distinguish customers who purchase occasionally from customers who repeatedly interact with the business.

### Business Insight

Customers with a high number of orders may demonstrate stronger engagement and greater potential lifetime value.

---

# 7. Analysis 3 — Customer Average Order Value

### Objective

Calculate the average revenue generated per order for each customer.

### Formula

```text
Average Order Value
=
Customer Lifetime Revenue / Total Orders
```

### Business Purpose

AOV helps determine whether a customer tends to make small or large purchases.

### Business Insight

A customer with fewer purchases but a high AOV can still be commercially valuable. Therefore, customer value should not be evaluated using purchase frequency alone.

---

# 8. Analysis 4 — Customer Lifetime Duration

### Objective

Measure how long customers have remained active between their first and last recorded purchases.

The analysis calculates:

```text
First Purchase Date
Last Purchase Date
Customer Lifetime Days
```

using:

```sql
DATEDIFF(MAX(order_date), MIN(order_date))
```

### Business Purpose

Customer lifetime duration helps understand the length of the customer's observed relationship with the business.

### Business Insight

Customers with longer purchase histories may represent established relationships and potential opportunities for retention and loyalty programs.

---

# 9. Analysis 5 — Customer Purchase Frequency

### Objective

Measure how frequently customers place orders relative to their observed customer lifetime.

The analysis calculates:

```text
Orders per Month
```

using the total number of orders and the customer's lifetime duration.

### Business Purpose

Purchase frequency helps identify highly active customers and customers with lower purchasing activity.

### Business Insight

Customers with high purchasing frequency can be targeted with loyalty benefits, subscription-style programs, cross-selling campaigns, and personalized recommendations.

---

# 10. Analysis 6 — Customer Revenue Ranking

### Objective

Rank customers according to their lifetime revenue.

The analysis uses:

```sql
DENSE_RANK() OVER (
    ORDER BY SUM(quantity * price) DESC
)
```

### Business Purpose

Revenue ranking makes it easier to identify the most financially valuable customers.

### Business Insight

Revenue rankings can support:

* VIP customer identification
* Account prioritization
* Retention strategies
* Personalized marketing
* Customer success programs

---

# 11. Analysis 7 — Top 10 Highest-Value Customers

### Objective

Identify the ten customers generating the highest lifetime revenue.

The analysis uses:

```sql
ORDER BY lifetime_revenue DESC
LIMIT 10
```

### Business Purpose

The top customers represent an important group for targeted customer relationship strategies.

### Business Insight

Businesses should focus on retaining their highest-value customers because losing a high-revenue customer can have a larger financial impact than losing a low-value customer.

---

# 12. Analysis 8 — Customer Revenue Contribution %

### Objective

Calculate the percentage of total revenue contributed by each customer.

### Formula

```text
Revenue Contribution %
=
Customer Lifetime Revenue
/
Total Customer Revenue
× 100
```

The analysis uses the SQL window function:

```sql
SUM(lifetime_revenue) OVER ()
```

### Business Purpose

Revenue contribution helps understand how strongly individual customers contribute to overall business revenue.

### Business Insight

This analysis can reveal whether revenue is broadly distributed across customers or concentrated among a smaller group of high-value customers.

---

# 13. Analysis 9 — Customer Value Classification

### Objective

Classify customers into value segments based on lifetime revenue.

The classification used in this project is:

| Lifetime Revenue | Segment  |
| ---------------: | -------- |
|        ≥ 100,000 | Platinum |
|         ≥ 50,000 | Gold     |
|         ≥ 20,000 | Silver   |
|         < 20,000 | Standard |

### Business Purpose

Customer segmentation makes customer-level revenue easier to interpret and use for business decision-making.

### Business Insight

Different customer segments can receive different strategies.

**Platinum customers**

* Premium loyalty benefits
* Dedicated support
* Exclusive offers

**Gold customers**

* Retention incentives
* Personalized promotions
* Cross-selling opportunities

**Silver customers**

* Upselling campaigns
* Product recommendations
* Loyalty incentives

**Standard customers**

* Engagement campaigns
* First-to-repeat purchase strategies
* Promotional offers

> Note: These thresholds are business classification rules used for analysis and are not automatically derived from the dataset.

# 15. Analysis 11 — Revenue Concentration Analysis

### Objective

Analyze the cumulative contribution of customers to total revenue.

The analysis calculates:

```text
Revenue Rank
Revenue Percentage
Cumulative Revenue Percentage
```

using SQL window functions.

### Business Purpose

Revenue concentration analysis helps determine how quickly total revenue accumulates as customers are ranked from highest to lowest revenue.

### Business Insight

If a relatively small number of customers account for a large proportion of revenue, the business may have significant customer concentration risk.

At the same time, these customers represent an important retention opportunity.

# 18. Business Insights

The Day 36 analysis provides several important business perspectives.

### 1. Customer value is different from customer count

A large number of customers does not necessarily mean that revenue is evenly distributed.

Some customers may contribute significantly more revenue than others.

### 2. High-value customers should receive special attention

Customers generating substantial lifetime revenue should be prioritized for retention and relationship-building activities.

### 3. Purchase frequency provides an additional value signal

Two customers may generate similar revenue but have very different purchasing patterns.

Combining revenue with order frequency provides a better understanding of customer behavior.

### 4. Average Order Value helps identify purchasing strength

Customers with high AOV may be strong candidates for premium products and upselling strategies.

### 5. Customer lifetime duration provides relationship context

Revenue becomes more meaningful when considered alongside the length of the customer's relationship with the business.

### 6. Revenue concentration can reveal business risk

If revenue is heavily concentrated among a small number of customers, the business should maintain strong retention strategies for those customers.

### 7. Customer segmentation enables personalized strategies

Dividing customers into Platinum, Gold, Silver, and Standard segments allows businesses to create targeted customer strategies.
