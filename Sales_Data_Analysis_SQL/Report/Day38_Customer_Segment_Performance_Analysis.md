## 4. Analytical Approach

Day 38 uses the RFM segmentation developed during Day 37.

The analysis follows these major steps:

### Step 1 — Calculate Recency

Recency measures how recently a customer placed an order.

Customers with fewer days since their latest purchase are considered more recent.

### Step 2 — Calculate Frequency

Frequency measures the total number of orders placed by a customer.

Customers with more orders have higher purchase frequency.

### Step 3 — Calculate Monetary Value

Monetary value represents the total revenue generated by a customer.

Revenue is calculated using:

`quantity × product price`

### Step 4 — Generate RFM Scores

Customers are assigned scores from 1 to 5 using `NTILE(5)`.

Higher scores represent stronger customer performance.

### Step 5 — Assign Customer Segments

Customers are classified using their RFM scores.

The main segments are:

* Champions
* Loyal Customers
* New Customers
* At-Risk Customers
* Lost Customers
* Potential Customers

### Step 6 — Analyze Segment Performance

The RFM segments are then compared using:

* Customer count
* Revenue
* Orders
* Average revenue
* Average order value
* Purchase frequency
* Revenue contribution
* Customer ranking

## Purpose

This analysis determines how many customers belong to each RFM segment.

The customer count helps understand the size of every customer group.

### Key Metrics

* RFM Segment
* Number of Customers

### Business Importance

Customer distribution helps businesses identify:

* Largest customer segments

A large At-Risk or Lost customer population may indicate the need for customer retention campaigns.

---

# 6. Analysis 2 — Revenue by RFM Segment

## Purpose

This analysis calculates the total revenue generated by each customer segment.

Revenue is calculated from:

`order_items.quantity × products.price`

### Key Metrics

* RFM Segment
* Total Revenue

### Business Importance

This analysis identifies the customer segments that contribute the most revenue.

A segment with fewer customers can still be extremely valuable if those customers generate high revenue.

---

# 7. Analysis 3 — Orders by RFM Segment

## Purpose

This analysis compares the total number of orders generated by each RFM segment.

### Key Metrics

* RFM Segment
* Total Orders

### Business Importance

Order volume helps measure customer engagement.

Segments with higher order counts generally demonstrate stronger purchasing activity.

This metric can be compared with revenue to determine whether a segment has:

* High order volume and high revenue
* High order volume but low revenue
* Low order volume but high revenue
* Low order volume and low revenue

---

# 8. Analysis 4 — Average Revenue per Customer by Segment

## Purpose

This analysis calculates the average revenue generated per customer within each RFM segment.

### Formula

`Average Revenue per Customer = Total Segment Revenue / Number of Customers`

### Business Importance

This metric provides a better understanding of customer value within each segment.

For example, a segment may contain fewer customers but generate significantly higher revenue per customer.

Such a segment can receive greater attention from the business.

---

# 9. Analysis 5 — Average Order Value by Segment

## Purpose

This analysis compares customer spending behavior across RFM segments.

Average Order Value indicates how much customers typically spend per order.

### Business Importance

A higher average order value may indicate:

* Larger purchases
* Premium product purchases
* Stronger customer trust
* Better cross-selling opportunities

A lower average order value may indicate opportunities for:

* Upselling
* Cross-selling
* Product bundles
* Minimum-order promotions

---

# 10. Analysis 6 — Purchase Frequency by Segment

## Purpose

This analysis compares the average number of orders placed by customers in each segment.

### Key Metric

Average orders per customer.

### Business Importance

Purchase frequency is an important indicator of customer engagement.

Segments with high purchase frequency are valuable because they demonstrate repeated customer behavior.

Low-frequency segments may require:

* Re-engagement campaigns
* Personalized offers
* Product recommendations
* Loyalty programs

---

# 11. Analysis 7 — Revenue Contribution Percentage by Segment

## Purpose

This analysis calculates the percentage of total company revenue contributed by each customer segment.

### Formula

`Revenue Contribution % = Segment Revenue / Total Revenue × 100`

### Business Importance

Revenue contribution helps determine how dependent the business is on specific customer segments.

For example:

* Champions may generate a large percentage of revenue.
* Loyal Customers may provide stable recurring revenue.
* At-Risk Customers may represent revenue that could potentially be recovered.
* Lost Customers may represent previously generated revenue that is no longer active.

# 18. Business Insights

Day 38 provides several important business perspectives.

## 18.1 Customer Count Does Not Equal Customer Value

The largest customer segment may not necessarily generate the highest revenue.

Businesses should evaluate both:

* Customer quantity
* Customer value

---

## 18.2 Champions Are High-Priority Customers

Champion customers demonstrate strong recent activity, purchase frequency, and monetary value.

They should receive strong retention and loyalty strategies.

---

## 18.3 At-Risk Customers Represent a Recovery Opportunity

At-Risk customers can represent significant recoverable revenue.

Businesses should identify high-value At-Risk customers and target them with personalized retention campaigns.

---

## 18.4 Revenue Concentration Should Be Monitored

If a large percentage of revenue comes from a small customer segment, the business may become dependent on those customers.

Customer diversification and retention strategies can reduce this risk.

---

## 18.5 Average Revenue per Customer Improves Segment Comparison

Total revenue alone can be misleading.

Average revenue per customer helps determine which segments contain the highest-value individual customers.

---

## 18.6 Purchase Frequency Shows Customer Engagement

Customers who purchase frequently are more engaged with the business.

Increasing purchase frequency can be an important growth strategy.
