# Day 72 — Customer Segmentation and Revenue Contribution Analysis Using SQL

## 1. Project Overview

Day 72 focuses on customer segmentation and revenue contribution analysis using MySQL 8.0. The project analyzes customer purchasing behavior, ranks customers by revenue, calculates individual revenue contribution, and compares revenue across customer segments.

The objective is to identify high-value customers, understand how sales revenue is distributed, and generate insights that can support customer retention and marketing decisions.

## 2. Objectives

- Calculate customer-level revenue and purchase summaries.
- Segment customers according to relative revenue contribution.
- Calculate each customer's percentage contribution to total revenue.
- Rank customers based on revenue.
- Analyze cumulative revenue contribution.
- Compare revenue performance across customer segments.
- Identify the top revenue-contributing customers.
- Support data-driven customer relationship and marketing strategies.

## 4. Analysis Performed

### Query 1: Customer Purchase and Revenue Summary

Calculated customer-level metrics, including:
- Total orders
- Total units purchased
- Total revenue
- Average order value

**Business application:** Helps compare customer purchasing activity and identify customers generating more revenue.

### Query 2: Revenue-Based Customer Segmentation

Used the `NTILE(3)` window function to divide customers with positive revenue into three approximately equal-sized groups:
- High Value
- Medium Value
- Low Value

**Business application:** Helps businesses tailor promotions, loyalty programs, and customer engagement strategies to different revenue groups.

**Methodology note:** These are relative segments based on the dataset, not fixed monetary thresholds.

### Query 3: Customer Revenue Contribution Percentage

Calculated each customer's percentage contribution to total revenue.

**Business application:** Shows which customers contribute the largest shares of sales revenue.

### Query 4: Customer Revenue Ranking

Used the `DENSE_RANK()` window function to rank customers according to total revenue.

**Business application:** Helps prioritize high-revenue customers for relationship management and retention activities.

### Query 5: Cumulative Revenue Contribution

Calculated cumulative revenue and the cumulative percentage of revenue as customers are ordered from highest to lowest revenue.

**Business application:** Helps evaluate how much of the total revenue is generated by the leading customers and supports customer concentration analysis.

### Query 6: Revenue Summary by Customer Segment

Compared customer counts, total segment revenue, and average customer revenue across the three segments.

**Business application:** Helps assess the relative financial importance of each customer segment.

### Query 7: Segment Share of Total Revenue

Calculated each segment's percentage share of total revenue.

**Business application:** Helps management understand how overall sales revenue is distributed across customer segments.

### Query 8: Top 10 Revenue-Contributing Customers

Identified the highest-ranked customers and displayed their order counts, purchased units, total revenue, and revenue rank.

**Business application:** Helps identify priority customers for retention programs and personalized marketing.

## 6. Revenue Calculation Method

Revenue is calculated using the quantity of products purchased multiplied by the corresponding product price:

`Revenue = SUM(order_items.quantity * products.price)`

The analysis joins customers to orders, order items, and products using their corresponding identifiers.

**Important limitation:** The calculation uses the product prices currently stored in the `products` table. If product prices have changed since an order was placed, the calculated revenue may differ from historical transaction revenue.

## 8. Limitations

- Customer segments are based on relative revenue ranking rather than predetermined business thresholds.
- Customers with zero revenue are excluded from the revenue-based segments and positive-revenue contribution rankings.
- The number of customers in each segment may differ slightly when the total number of customers is not divisible by three.
- Revenue calculations depend on the accuracy of order, order-item, product, and customer records.
- Current product prices may not reflect historical selling prices or discounts.
- Revenue contribution alone does not measure profitability, customer acquisition cost, or customer lifetime value.
- Actual business conclusions must be based on the query results obtained from the database.
