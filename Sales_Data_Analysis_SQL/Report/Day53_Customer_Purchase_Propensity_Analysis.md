📊 Day 53 — Customer Purchase Propensity Analysis
📌 Introduction

Day 53 focuses on Customer Purchase Propensity Analysis using MySQL.

The objective is to analyze customer purchasing behavior and develop a rule-based Customer Purchase Propensity Score using purchase frequency, purchase recency, and customer revenue.

This analysis extends the customer analytics work completed in previous days and moves the project toward customer targeting, segmentation, and business decision support.

🎯 Objectives

The main objectives of Day 53 are:

Analyze customer purchase frequency.
Calculate customer revenue contribution.
Measure customer purchase recency.
Calculate average time between purchases.
Classify customers based on purchasing behavior.
Develop a customer purchase propensity score.
Classify customers according to their propensity.
Rank customers based on purchasing activity.
Create a consolidated customer propensity summary.
Demonstrate how SQL can support customer targeting decisions.

📊 Analyses Performed
1. Total Orders per Customer

Measures the total number of orders placed by each customer.

Business Purpose

Helps identify customers with frequent purchasing behavior and distinguish repeat customers from one-time customers.

2. Total Revenue per Customer

Calculates the total revenue generated by each customer.

Business Purpose

Helps identify customers contributing significant revenue to the business.

3. Average Order Value per Customer

Calculates the average revenue generated per customer order.

Business Purpose

Helps understand individual customer spending behavior.

4. Customer Purchase Recency

Calculates:

Days Since Last Purchase

using the customer's latest order date.

Business Purpose

Helps distinguish recently active customers from customers whose purchasing activity has become inactive.

5. Average Days Between Purchases

The LAG() window function is used to compare each customer's current purchase date with their previous purchase date.

LAG(order_date)
OVER (
    PARTITION BY customer_id
    ORDER BY order_date
)
Business Purpose

This helps measure customer purchase frequency and understand the typical time between purchases.

6. Customer Purchase Recency Classification

Customers are classified according to the number of days since their latest purchase.

Days Since Purchase	Classification
≤ 30	Highly Recent
≤ 60	Recent
≤ 90	Moderately Recent
> 90	Inactive
7. Customer Purchase Frequency Classification

Customers are classified based on their total number of orders.

Total Orders	Classification
10+	Very High Frequency
5–9	High Frequency
2–4	Repeat Customer
1	One-Time Customer
8. Customer Revenue Classification

Customers are classified according to their total revenue contribution.

Revenue	Classification
₹10,000+	High Value Customer
₹5,000–₹9,999	Medium Value Customer
₹1,000–₹4,999	Standard Value Customer
Below ₹1,000	Low Value Customer
⭐ 9. Customer Purchase Propensity Score

A rule-based scoring system combines three major customer signals:

Purchase Frequency
Condition	Score
10+ orders	+3
5–9 orders	+2
2–4 orders	+1
1 order	+0
Purchase Recency
Condition	Score
≤ 30 days	+3
≤ 60 days	+2
≤ 90 days	+1
> 90 days	+0
Customer Revenue
Condition	Score
₹10,000+	+3
₹5,000–₹9,999	+2
₹1,000–₹4,999	+1
Below ₹1,000	+0

The final score is:

Purchase Frequency Score
+
Purchase Recency Score
+
Revenue Score

The maximum possible score is:

9
🎯 10. Purchase Propensity Classification

Customers are classified using their total propensity score.

Score	Classification
7–9	Very High Purchase Propensity
5–6	High Purchase Propensity
3–4	Moderate Purchase Propensity
1–2	Low Purchase Propensity
0	Very Low Purchase Propensity
🏆 11. Customer Purchase Propensity Ranking

Customers are ranked using:

Total orders
Total revenue
Latest purchase date

The RANK() window function is used to create the customer ranking.

RANK() OVER (
    ORDER BY
        total_orders DESC,
        total_revenue DESC,
        last_purchase_date DESC
)
📋 12. Final Customer Purchase Propensity Summary

The final analysis combines:

Customer ID

Last purchase date
Days since last purchase
Purchase propensity score
Purchase propensity category

This creates a consolidated customer-level view for business analysis.

⚠️ Methodology Limitation

The Day 53 propensity model is a rule-based SQL scoring system.

It is not a machine-learning model and the propensity score should not be interpreted as a statistically validated probability of future purchase.

The score is based on predefined business rules using:

Purchase frequency
Purchase recency
Customer revenue

A future version could extend this analysis with machine-learning techniques such as:

Logistic Regression
Decision Trees
Random Forest
Gradient Boosting
Additional customer behavioral features
