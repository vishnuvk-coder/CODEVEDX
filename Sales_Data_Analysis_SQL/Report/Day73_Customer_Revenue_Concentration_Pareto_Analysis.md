# Day 73: Customer Revenue Concentration and Pareto (80/20) Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

This project analyzes customer revenue using MySQL 8.0 to understand how sales revenue is distributed among customers. It applies customer ranking, revenue contribution percentages, cumulative revenue analysis, and the Pareto principle.

## 2. Objectives

- Calculate total revenue generated by each customer.
- Rank customers based on revenue contribution.
- Calculate individual and cumulative revenue percentages.
- Compare the top 20% of purchasing customers with the remaining customers.
- Identify how many top customers are needed to reach at least 80% of total revenue.

## 3. Tools and SQL Concepts

**Tools:** MySQL 8.0, MySQL Workbench, VS Code, Git, and GitHub.

**SQL concepts used:**
- Aggregate functions: `SUM()` and `COUNT()`
- Joins across customers, orders, order items, and products
- Common Table Expressions (CTEs)
- Window functions: `ROW_NUMBER()`, `DENSE_RANK()`, and `SUM() OVER()`
- `CASE` expressions
- Percentage calculations and rounding

## 4. Analysis Performed

### Query 1: Customer Revenue Summary

Calculated each customer's total revenue and number of orders.

### Query 2: Customer Revenue Ranking

Ranked customers by total revenue and calculated each customer's percentage contribution to overall revenue.

### Query 3: Cumulative Revenue Contribution

Calculated the running total of customer revenue and its cumulative percentage of overall revenue.

### Query 4: Top 20% Customer Comparison

Separated purchasing customers into the top 20% and the remaining 80%, then compared their customer counts, revenue totals, and revenue shares.

### Query 5: 80% Revenue Target

Identified the first customer position at which cumulative revenue reaches or exceeds 80% of total revenue.

## 5. Business Value

This analysis can help businesses:

- Identify customers who contribute substantially to revenue.
- Prioritize customer retention and relationship management.
- Understand whether sales depend heavily on a small group of customers.
- Support customer loyalty and targeted marketing strategies.
- Monitor revenue concentration and potential customer dependency risks.

## 7. Limitations

- The analysis includes customers with qualifying order and order-item records; customers with no purchases are not included.
- Revenue is calculated as quantity multiplied by the current product price. Historical product prices are not available in this calculation.
- The 80/20 principle is a business hypothesis, not a guaranteed result.
- The top 20% grouping uses the number of purchasing customers, rounded up to a whole customer.
- The results depend on the completeness and accuracy of the underlying database.
