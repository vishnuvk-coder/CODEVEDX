📈 Customer Revenue Analysis

Customer-level revenue was calculated by combining customer, order, and order-item information.

The analysis measures:

Total orders
Total units purchased
Total customer revenue

The basic revenue calculation is:

Quantity × Unit Price

Customer revenue is then aggregated to create a customer-level revenue profile.

📊 Customer Revenue Ranking

Customers were ranked according to their total revenue.

The ranking uses:

ROW_NUMBER() OVER (
    ORDER BY total_revenue DESC
)

This produces a revenue ranking from the highest-revenue customer to the lowest-revenue customer.

The ranking provides the foundation for the quartile analysis.

🔢 Customer Revenue Quartiles

Customers were divided into four revenue-based groups using:

NTILE(4) OVER (
    ORDER BY total_revenue DESC
)

The four quartiles represent approximately equal-sized customer groups based on revenue ranking.

Quartile	Customer Group
1	Top revenue quartile
2	Upper-middle revenue quartile
3	Lower-middle revenue quartile
4	Bottom revenue quartile

Quartile 1 contains customers ranked toward the highest end of customer revenue, while Quartile 4 contains customers ranked toward the lowest end.

👥 Customer Count by Quartile

The analysis calculates the number of customers assigned to each revenue quartile.

This helps determine how the customer population is distributed across the four revenue groups.

Because NTILE(4) divides rows into four groups as evenly as possible, the number of customers in each quartile can differ slightly when the total customer count is not divisible by four.

📊 Revenue Contribution by Quartile

Each quartile's revenue contribution is calculated as:

Quartile Revenue
/
Total Customer Revenue
× 100

This measures the percentage of total customer revenue generated by each revenue group.

The calculation helps compare the revenue importance of the four customer segments.

📈 Average Revenue per Customer

The analysis calculates the average revenue generated by customers within each quartile.

AVG(total_revenue)

This allows comparison of customer value between the top, middle, and bottom revenue groups.

The top revenue quartile is expected to contain customers with higher revenue values because the quartiles are created from revenue ranking.

📦 Orders and Units by Quartile

📈 Cumulative Revenue Contribution

Cumulative revenue is calculated using a window function:

SUM(quartile_revenue) OVER (
    ORDER BY revenue_quartile
    ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING
    AND CURRENT ROW
)

The cumulative calculation shows how revenue accumulates from the highest-revenue quartile toward the lowest-revenue quartile.

The corresponding cumulative percentage is calculated as:

Cumulative Revenue
/
Total Revenue
× 100

This provides a simplified view of the customer revenue distribution curve.

⚠️ Methodology Limitation

The Day 61 analysis describes historical customer revenue distribution in the available sales dataset.

NTILE(4) creates four revenue-ranked customer groups. It does not mean that each quartile contributes exactly 25% of revenue.

The analysis does not claim that:

Each quartile generates exactly 25% of revenue.
Customer behavior will remain unchanged.
Historical revenue guarantees future performance.
Quartile membership predicts future customer behavior.
The quartile classification is a machine-learning prediction.
Revenue distribution is statistically guaranteed.

The quartile framework is used as a business-analysis method for understanding customer revenue distribution.
